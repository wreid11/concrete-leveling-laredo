9 Questions to Ask Before You Sign a Leveling Quote

Laredo Concrete Leveling — A free checklist for homeowners

1
What method will you use—mudjacking or polyurethane foam?
Different methods have different lifespans, costs, and best uses. Mudjacking is cheaper but heavier; foam cures fast but costs more. A good contractor will recommend the right one for your slab and soil.
2
How many holes do you need to drill, and where will they be?
Fewer, well-placed holes mean fewer patches and a better finish. Ask to see a diagram. Holes drilled in high-traffic areas need invisible patches.
3
What's included in the price—material, labor, and patch?
Some quotes hide the patch cost or require a separate service. Get a clear line-item breakdown. Patches should match or be invisible.
4
How long will the work take, and when can I use the slab again?
Most leveling jobs finish in 2–4 hours. Mudjacking may need 12–24 hours before heavy traffic; foam is usually ready in 1 hour. Know your downtime.
5
Do you carry liability and workers' comp insurance?
Ask for proof. If they hit a gas line or get injured on your property, insurance protects you. No insurance = huge risk.
6
How long will the repair last?
Mudjacking typically lasts 8–10 years; foam often longer. Neither stops future soil movement—but both fix the current settled slab. Ask what happens if it settles again.
7
What's your warranty, and does it cover future settling?
Warranties vary from 6 months to 5 years. Know what you're covered for. Most contractors won't warranty against future soil movement—that's normal.
8
Are there any add-on costs after you start?
Ask about unexpected fees: extra material, extended labor, subcontractors, or permit costs. A clear upfront quote prevents surprises.
9
Can I call references, and how many leveling jobs have you done in Laredo?
Local contractors know Laredo's clay soil better. References tell you if they finish on time, clean up, and stand behind their work.

Pro Tip: Get at least two quotes. Leveling costs vary widely based on slab size, depth of settlement, and method. The cheapest quote isn't always the best—ask *why* one quote is higher.
