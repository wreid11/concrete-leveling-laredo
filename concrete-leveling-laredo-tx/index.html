What Is Concrete Leveling?

Concrete leveling raises a sunken slab back to its original position instead of demolishing and repouring it. A contractor drills small holes through the surface, then pumps material underneath to fill voids and lift the slab: a sand-cement slurry (mudjacking) or expanding polyurethane foam (polyjacking). The holes get patched, and the concrete is usable again within hours to a day.

It matters in Laredo for one big reason: the soil. South Texas clay shrinks during long dry spells and swells after heavy rain, and that shrink-swell cycle moves the ground under your slab. After a Laredo summer with water restrictions, it's common to see driveways and walkways settle at the edges while the middle stays high. Leveling corrects the result without paying for replacement.

Signs You Need Concrete Leveling

Call it early and leveling is a small job. Watch for:

  • Water pooling on a driveway or patio after rain — a sunken slab holds water against the surface
  • Trip edges where one sidewalk section sits higher than the next
  • Gaps between the driveway and the garage floor or foundation
  • Cracks that step up or down from one side to the other
  • A porch or steps that have dropped away from the house

If a slab is crumbling apart or heaving upward from tree roots, leveling may not be the right fix — that's what the free quote is for. A vetted contractor will tell you honestly whether your slab is a leveling job or a replacement job.

Concrete Leveling FAQ

How much does concrete leveling cost in Laredo TX?

Most jobs land between $3 and $25 per square foot depending on the method — mudjacking on the low end, polyjacking on the high end. A typical residential driveway runs $900–$3,000. Your exact price depends on slab size, how far it has sunk, and how much material the voids need.

Is mudjacking or polyjacking better for South Texas soil?

Both work. Mudjacking is cheaper and adds weight; polyfoam is lighter, cures in about an hour, and resists water. In Laredo's shrink-swell clay, most contractors recommend polyfoam for thinner slabs and precision lifts, mudjacking for heavy driveways where budget matters. Your quote will recommend one — ask why.

How long does the work take?

Most residential jobs finish in 2–4 hours. Mudjacked slabs can take light traffic the same day; polyfoam is usually ready within an hour of the lift. Replacement, by comparison, is a multi-day job with cure time.

Is leveling cheaper than replacing the concrete?

Usually, yes — expect to pay roughly a third to half of replacement cost for a sound slab. Leveling makes sense when the concrete is intact but settled. If the slab is badly cracked or crumbling, replacement is the honest answer, and a good contractor will say so.

How long does concrete leveling last?

Mudjacking commonly lasts 8–10 years; polyfoam often longer because it doesn't break down or wash out. Neither stops future soil movement — in Laredo's climate, keeping the soil moisture consistent around your slabs is what protects the fix.
